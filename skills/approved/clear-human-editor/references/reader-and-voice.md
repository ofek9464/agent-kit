# Reader and author voice

Read for substantial prose revision or when a passage feels lifeless, generic, or hard to follow. Apply within the edit and review boundaries in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). This is editorial guidance, not a required interview or a fixed narrative formula.

## Identify what the passage owes its reader

Infer who reads it, what they already know, and what they should understand, decide, or be able to do afterwards. A technical examiner may understand engineering while knowing nothing about this project's choices. Distinguish missing context from context the reader already has. Reuse the user's comments and previous decisions; do not ask the same discovery questions for every section.

Diagnose the main problem before editing words: missing substance, an unclear line of reasoning, the wrong register, or repetitive sentence patterns. If an engaging explanation needs a fact or experience only the author can provide, ask a focused popup question and continue unaffected work. Keep the gap outside the final prose until resolved; a true plain sentence is preferable to a vivid invented one.

## Develop the thought

Give consequential choices their supported reasons. Explain what a comparison tests before asking the reader to interpret its results. Connect adjacent facts with their actual relationship: cause, contrast, condition, example, or implication. Do not add a causal link merely because it makes the narrative smoother.

Interest can come from a practical problem, a meaningful tradeoff, an unexpected documented result, or a question the evidence will answer. Choose the opening that serves this passage. A methods section can begin directly with the setup; an abstract should deliver the required purpose, method, result, and conclusion without delaying them for suspense. Do not add a familiar-life scene, rhetorical question, anecdote, or concluding lesson to every section.

Cut repetition that adds nothing. Keep or add explanation when it helps the reader understand a mechanism, interpret a number, or see why a decision matters. Do not replace technical precision with an analogy; use an accurate analogy only if it helps this reader and keep its limits clear.

## Make wording concrete

When a phrase names an abstraction, ask what actually happens and to whom: what changes, what is measured, or what the choice permits. Prefer that explanation when it helps this reader. Keep an exact technical term when it carries a necessary distinction; explain it rather than replacing it with a loose everyday synonym.

Inspect phrases such as "שיפור של ממש", "תרומה משמעותית", "תמונה אחרת", and "חלוקה אחרת של הזמן" in context. They are diagnostic clues, not banned words. If the phrase stands in for missing information, name the observed difference or supported consequence. Preserve the original strength of the claim: do not turn ordinary significance into statistical significance or invent a threshold, cause, or measurement.

Choose wording that sounds plausible when the author explains this work to the intended reader. Avoid importing product, marketing, bureaucratic, or translated phrasing into an academic report merely to make it warmer. For example, inspect whether "חוויה שרצינו לשפר" actually describes the author's intended user-experience goal or whether the source identifies a concrete problem to reduce. Do not replace it mechanically or attribute a new motive.

## Manage the reader's load

Check the paragraph as well as its sentences. A series of individually readable sentences can still overload the reader with new controllers, baselines, scenarios, training runs, and measures. Introduce a term where its purpose is understandable, and keep the detail needed to interpret it close by. For a constructed measure, use its precise definition; "מדד הולכי הרגל" does not automatically mean mean pedestrian waiting time.

Arrange explanations around the comparison or question the reader is following. Separate setup, result, and interpretation where mixing them obscures the argument. In an abstract, select details that carry the purpose, method, main findings, and limits; place secondary breakdowns in the body when restructuring is authorized. Never remove a qualification or conflicting result just to reduce density. For a local polish, fix local wording and flag larger structural issues outside the revision rather than silently expanding scope.

There is no fixed quota for terms, numbers, or sentence length. Judge whether the reader can follow the actual relationships and retain the main point.

## Preserve a recognisable author

Use the author's samples and approved revisions to identify vocabulary, rhythm, directness, formality, and preferred ways of explaining. Preserve distinctive phrases when they carry meaning or voice. Do not copy factual content from a style sample into another passage. Treat user comments as evidence of preferences, not automatically as publishable sentences.

In project reports, let documented actions and judgments carry the authors' presence: what they compared, why a choice was made, what failed, and what the evidence changed. First-person usage follows the assignment and authors. Neutral technical prose can still show attentive reasoning. Do not manufacture personal reactions or infer effort, frustration, surprise, or motivation from a result alone.

Vary sentence and paragraph shape to fit the thought. Keep a longer sentence when its relationships are clear; use a short sentence when a new point deserves emphasis. Do not enforce sentence-length quotas, artificial imperfections, slang, jokes, or punctuation bans to perform humanness. Apply unslop without stripping useful character.

## Read in sequence

Read the changed passage and its transitions as the intended reader encountering them for the first time. Locate where the reader lacks a referent, cannot see why a detail matters, receives too many new terms, or loses the question being answered. Fix the material gap rather than describing the prose as merely boring. This internal reader simulation is an editorial heuristic, not evidence of a real reader test.

Check vague evaluations, undefined measures, and the number of new ideas carried by each paragraph. Resolve them from the source without diluting precision. Check claims and qualifications against the source, then make one final voice and rhythm pass. Stop when the requested scope and content checks pass; repeated cosmetic passes can flatten the prose.

## Illustrative Hebrew examples

These are examples of reasoning and register, not user-approved voice samples or claims about a particular project. Use their facts and rationale only when supported by that project's source.

**Explain a comparison's purpose.**

Thin: "השווינו בקר לומד לטיימר קבוע ולטיימר מכויל."

Developed: "השוואה לטיימר קבוע מאפשרת לבדוק אם הבקר הלומד מקצר את ההמתנה ביחס לתזמון שנבחר מראש. הוספת טיימר מכויל מאפשרת לבדוק כמה מהפער נשאר כשהתזמון הקבוע מותאם לתרחיש."

The added explanation gives the comparison a reason. It does not claim a measured advantage before presenting evidence.

**Explain a technical idea through its consequence.**

Thin: "רמזור מחלק משאב מוגבל: זמן ירוק."

Developed: "כשמאריכים את האור הירוק לכיוון אחד, הכיוון האחר ממתין יותר. לכן צריך לבחון איך חלוקת הזמן משפיעה על ההמתנה בכל הכיוונים."

The relationship makes the idea concrete. Retain the actual controller's constraints and measures when adapting it.

**Replace vague evaluation with a supported comparison.**

Vague: "הבקר השיג שיפור של ממש."

Specific, only when these values are supplied: "זמן ההמתנה הממוצע ירד מ־30 ל־27 שניות."

These numbers are synthetic example data, not report results. If no magnitude or criterion is available, preserve the supported claim plainly and ask or flag the missing basis. A numerical reduction alone does not establish statistical significance.

**Introduce the purpose of a new baseline.**

Dense: "השווינו את הבקר לטבלת משכים קבועה לכל צומת ומופע."

Explained, when the method supports it: "כדי לבדוק אם התגובה לתנועה מוסיפה לשיפור, השווינו את הבקר גם לטבלה שמקצה מראש משך ירוק לכל צומת ומופע."

The purpose makes the term easier to follow. Explain the table's construction elsewhere as needed; do not infer it from the name alone.

When collecting approved voice examples, keep the original, the preferred revision, the reason for the preference, and the applicable register together. Extract a transferable preference rather than a mandatory replacement phrase. A displayed or preselected popup choice is not approval; only the user's submitted answer counts.

When the user approves a revision as representative of their voice, reuse that preference in the current work. Save a durable sample only in a user-authorized location, with enough context to distinguish voice from project-specific facts.

> במהלך הפרויקט רצינו לבחון מספר שיטות שונות של בקרת רמוזרים בשביל שיהיה לנו השוואה אמיתית

> ובסוף רצינו לראות מה יהיה הבבדילם בכמה נצליח לשפר בכל שלב ואם השיוניי הזה יהיה שווה את הסיבוכוית כלומר אם סקרפיט יתן לנו 80 אחוז מהביצועים ב10 מאמץ אולי זה יהיה לנו שווה
> ככל שהתקדמנו בסקריפט הבנו שצריך להתייחס ליותר פרמטרים ומפה הבנו באמת שזה חייב להיות ML שלקוח ולומד את הצמטטים ואת כל הפרמטרים שלהם ולפי זה עושה החלטות בזמן אמת

Transferable preferences inferred from this sample:
- Explain the team's thinking as a connected progression. First establish a baseline, then explain what an added approach is meant to contribute. Preserve causal reasoning and motivation rather than converting everything into isolated polished declarations.
- Use ordinary connective language such as "קודם", "רצינו לראות", "בהמשך", and "כלומר" where it helps follow the thought. Do not enforce these expressions or repeat "רצינו" mechanically.
- Keep the practical question visible: how much improvement each approach adds and whether it justifies the effort and complexity. The author welcomes a concrete illustrative comparison to explain that tradeoff.
- Preserve the direct first-person team voice and space to develop the explanation. Correct spelling, agreement, and overloaded sentences without sanding the explanation into generic formal prose. Typographical errors are not part of the preferred voice.

Evidence and interpretation limits:
- The 80-percent performance / 10-percent effort comparison is hypothetical motivation, not a measured finding; make that status explicit if used. Clarify the quantity rather than silently assigning a unit to "ב10 מאמץ".
- The preference for ML over an LLM is an author-reported design rationale. The report states no measured LLM comparison; do not claim demonstrated superiority or faster response times without new evidence.
- "זה חייב להיות ML" expresses the author's reasoning at that point, not proof that a script cannot handle additional parameters. Preserve the decision as a decision and distinguish it from a demonstrated technical necessity.
- Do not infer a precise meaning for garbled phrases such as "שטתנר רוחבי". Omit a nonessential unclear phrase or ask a targeted clarification when it changes the content.
- Do not use the sample's general statement about today's traffic lights as evidence about real installations. Keep claims about the project's fixed-timer baseline grounded in the report and sources.

For further voice calibration, prefer one concrete project topic at a time and a free-text answer over choosing among model-written sentences. The popup closed immediately for this user; if it remains unusable, ask in chat rather than assuming the accepted tool call was visible. Update durable preferences only from actual answers.
