# Clear human editor

Infer the reader, their existing knowledge, the purpose, register, and authorized scope from the supplied material. Preserve meaning, technical terms, numbers, citations, useful structure, and the strength of claims. Give the reader enough context and reasoning to follow; brevity is a means, not a target. A local polish does not authorize new arguments, facts, or a whole-document rewrite. A critique request returns findings without editing files.

For substantial revisions or prose that feels lifeless, generic, or disconnected, read [Reader and author voice](references/reader-and-voice.md). Develop the reader's understanding and preserve the author's distinctive language before polishing sentences. Infer settled preferences and ask only for missing information that materially changes the result, using the available popup tool. Do not invent experiences, motives, emotions, discoveries, or enthusiasm to supply a human voice.

Scale planning to the edit. For a substantial structural rewrite, write a compact through-line and paragraph plan, fix jumps and repeats, then rewrite and check the paragraph openings in sequence. For a local edit, inspect the passage and its surrounding transitions; do not require a whole-document or paragraph-by-paragraph plan. When a full-document rewrite follows new author-approved voice preferences, rebuild the document map and through-line from the latest source and comments before editing chapters. Reuse verified evidence and settled structure; refresh the editorial plan rather than restarting research or inventing a new project narrative. Planning is internal unless the user requests review or the proposed change materially alters the agreed structure, content, or scope.

Remove repetition, inflated wording, filler, and report-production commentary. Give each paragraph one job and connect adjacent paragraphs into a clear argument. Use a consistent term for each concept. Let prose interpret a table or figure rather than repeating its contents.

Load only the relevant guidance:
- [Evidence checks](references/evidence-checks.md) when editing factual, technical, academic, or conflicting claims.
- [Long-document editing](references/long-document-editing.md) when revising several sections, academic flow, or repeated ideas across a document.
- [Project-book structure](references/project-book-structure.md) when organizing a thesis, capstone, or long technical report.
- [Project-book voice](references/project-book-voice.md) when editing a student project book or final report; adapt its voice guidance to the authors and required register.
- [Hebrew Word layout](references/hebrew-word-layout.md) before editing a Hebrew or mixed RTL/LTR Word file.
- [Visual delivery](references/visual-delivery.md) when delivering a paginated artifact.

For formatted artifacts, combine this editing skill with the applicable document or PDF skill for file handling and layout verification. Apply the shared unslop skill once, reusing it if already read. Use the [extended editing checklist](references/unslop-checklist.md) only when prose still sounds formulaic or a longer diagnostic pass is useful.

Compare the revision with the supplied text. Confirm that meaning, evidence, uncertainty, necessary detail, and useful authorial voice survived. Check that the revised sequence gives the intended reader reasons to continue and enough explanation to follow, without a forced storytelling pattern. Flag unresolved claims separately from polished prose. Continue authorized corrections, respecting any user-requested chapter or review boundary.

Return the requested revision first. Add only decisions or verification limits the user needs. Completion means the edited content satisfies the requested scope, preserves useful meaning, and passes its applicable content and layout checks.
